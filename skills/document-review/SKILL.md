# /document-review

## Purpose
Reviews any document—proposal, plan, design doc, PRD, or policy—for completeness, feasibility, risk awareness, and strategic alignment.  
It ensures the author has examined prior art, surfaced measurable risks, identified dependencies, and communicated clearly with affected stakeholders.

## When to use
Use whenever a new document is shared internally that describes work, architecture, operations, or strategy, before sign‑off or publication.

## How to Review
1. Read the document end‑to‑end to grasp its intent and audience.
2. Identify its type (technical, strategic, operational, policy, etc.) to tailor your lens.
3. Evaluate along each review dimension below.
4. Highlight strengths, missing context, or unclear assumptions.
5. Produce structured feedback grouped by category.

## Checklist

**Context & Motivation**
- Does it define the problem, goals, and constraints clearly?
- Does it articulate the “why” behind the work?

**Prior Art & Precedent**
- Have similar initiatives or solutions been explored?
- Is prior art cited, contrasted, or synthesized to justify this approach?

**Feasibility & Resource Realism**
- Are scope, effort, and resources practical for the timeline?
- Are technical, financial, or staffing assumptions credible?

**Risks & Mitigations**
- Are risks explicitly named, qualified (likelihood, impact) or quantified?
- Are mitigations and contingencies described?

**Dependencies & Clashes**
- Are dependencies and potential conflicts identified?
- Do timelines overlap with other projects or commitments?

**Validation & Success Criteria**
- How will success be measured or verified?
- Are validation steps incremental or all‑at‑once?

**Stakeholders & Alignment**
- Are all impacted teams and decision‑makers identified?
- Is ownership clear for sign‑offs and next steps?

**Ethical & Societal Impact**
- Could this work introduce bias, privacy, or fairness issues?
- Does it consider impacts on users, partners, or internal culture?

**Communication Readiness**
- Is the document clear and structured for its audience?
- Are key messages, decisions, and action points easy to extract?

**Decision Clarity**
- Are decisions, open questions, and rationales explicit?
- Is there a clear go/no‑go or sign‑off signal?

**Next Steps**
- Are actions measurable, time‑bound, and assigned?
- Is there a visibility or reporting plan for progress?

Output: Structured review comments per category.
